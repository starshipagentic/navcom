# NavCom spatial navigation vision

## Purpose and status

Preserve the owner's working context across a day job, paid client work, entrepreneurial ventures, product suites, experiments and time. Returning to work should not require reconstructing thought from browser tabs and scattered conversations.

This records the owner's conceptual direction and supersedes the assistant's earlier speculative mappings. It defines the intended experience, without changing Starship's commissioning contract. Current implementation and verification are tracked in the [galactic implementation plan](galactic-implementation-plan.md); the vision alone is not evidence of completion.

## Galaxy and solar systems

The owner's galaxy represents their overall world of work, spanning their machines and locations. It is not tied to one Macintosh. Visiting a friend such as David, with his own companies and commitments, means visiting another galaxy.

Solar systems hold substantial commitments and product suites. Syra, the owner's W-2 day job, is one solar system. Other paid client engagements and entrepreneurial ventures have room for their own systems. These are substantial spaces with multiple products, not one small system per conversation.

Physical machines and cloud infrastructure form an underlying plane that sustains the universe—the owner likened it to dark matter. Conceptual geography does not depend on which machine hosts it. Actual storage, runtime location, selected copy and availability must remain inspectable. A Mac is not automatically a sun or planet; an SSD can embody a deliberately named starbase. Exact controls for inspecting this plane remain open.

## Products, experiments and places

A planet represents a product within a suite. In the Syra system, the B2B platform, the enterprise mobile application, and CarePlus must be distinguishable physical destinations, rather than disappear inside one generic project cluster. The owner's examples suggest separate planets; exact repository-to-product bindings need verification before implementation.

A planet has history. Its code, architecture, strategy and surrounding experiments evolve. A time slider reveals the solar system and planets at a selected period. Major product generations may warrant distinct planets linked through that history. Whether Syrenity 1 and Syrenity 2 are separate worlds or successive states must remain open until the owner's intended distinction is clarified. Both product evolution and explicit replacement must be representable.

Lab experiments are moons. Versions participate in this history and experimental geography, but not every routine version must automatically become a separate moon.

Landing on a planet brings its code and thinking into view. Cities, districts and recognizable facilities represent meaningful areas of the product: login and Cognito, role-based access control, Docebo integration, infrastructure, and other strategies or capabilities. These are useful places to inspect decisions, implementations, alternatives and related conversations. Exact boundaries between a city and district remain design work.

Searching within a district, project or product should feel like examining the place one has arrived at. Search highlights relevant locations and evidence without scrambling established geography. Location, selection, scope and return path must stay understandable.

## Stars

Stars are significant living sources of expertise. They can embody an agent, maintained codebase, repository, website and callable interfaces, supported by a physical or virtual machine. They accumulate expertise through fusion and can serve visitors across clients, projects, systems and galaxies.

Stars are their own kind of destination. Do not force one to be the owner's primary Mac, or require every work solar system to have an artificial expertise star merely to satisfy astronomy. A star may warrant its own surrounding system, but no fixed astronomical nesting is decided here.

Starforge technology can help fuse and improve work throughout the owner's galaxy; its use is not limited to creating public stars. A star can be private or shared. Visiting, searching, connecting, using capabilities and offering discoveries depend on the star's actual interfaces and access rules.

## Starbases and ships

A starbase is a navigable gathering, docking, storage or resource location. An attached SSD or other real location can embody a starbase and should appear on the chart. Starbases can hold ships; their earlier social and stewardship meanings remain relevant.

A starship is a coherent living working environment carrying the selected work forward: documented code, necessary configuration and dependencies, required credentials and access material, NavCom conversations, decisions, history, and working context. Access material is carried securely; the visual map exposes neither secret values nor credentials in links.

A ship can focus on an undertaking within a larger product. For example, a ship devoted to Syra B2B's CMS ACCESS work carries the context and access needed for that undertaking; it need not stand for the entire Syra suite.

The ship has a location in the conceptual geography, a point of view, a purpose and a recoverable working state. Boarding it should restore the context from which the captain can answer questions and advance the work. It can be positioned at a planet, district, experiment or other relevant destination while being physically hosted elsewhere.

An ordinary conversation is not automatically a complete ship. This NavCom design conversation would become part of a ship only when the necessary work, history, configuration and access are commissioned into a recoverable environment. A transcript label or a blank resumed terminal does not establish ship readiness.

Conversations are first-class contextual material available at places and aboard ships. Their exact visual representation remains open; parent/child conversation relationships do not automatically mean planet/moon relationships.

## Visual and interaction direction

Use the existing Starship art library for cities, settlements, ships, starbases, laboratories, docks, archive vaults and NavCom facilities. Their roles should correspond to meaningful destinations and capabilities.

Preserve a stable sense of place. Moving through time changes the historical view deliberately; searching does not randomly rearrange the world. Provide clear bearings and a dependable return to the previous location and working state. Voice and typing must operate on the same navigation context.

Acceptance journey: enter the Syra system, distinguish B2B, mobile and CarePlus, select a product and historical period, visit its authentication or security area, inspect related experiments and conversations, then board a relevant commissioned ship and recover its documented working viewpoint. Return to the prior destination without losing bearings. Actual access/readiness must be tested rather than inferred from the artwork.
